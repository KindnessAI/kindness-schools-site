What it is

A school runs on thousands of small interactions a day. Most of the kind ones go unnoticed, and most of the unkind ones get all the attention. Kindness Schools flips that ratio on purpose.

Students

A daily kindness streak

One small suggested act a day - sit with someone new at lunch, thank a custodian by name, help a classmate study. Students log what they did and keep a streak, like a reading log for being decent to each other.

Teachers

Two minutes, not a curriculum

A morning prompt for the classroom and a Friday wrap-up the teacher can read in two minutes. No lesson planning, no grading - the product does the lifting.

Families

The conversation at home

Parents see what their child did this week and get one suggested family act for the weekend. Kindness stops being a school assembly topic and becomes a dinner-table one.

How it works

Three loops, all running on the same engine.

Daily

Notice, act, log

Each morning every student gets one age-appropriate act. Younger kids draw or tap what they did; older students write a line. Thirty seconds, done during homeroom.

Weekly

Classrooms climb together

Acts roll up to the classroom, not just the child. A fifth-grade class can see it out-kind the sixth graders this week - cooperation first, friendly competition second.

Termly

The school sees its culture

Acts by theme - inclusion, gratitude, helping, repair after conflict. Principals finally get a number for the thing assemblies talk about, and can see which grades need a nudge.

What the school sees

A dashboard built for a principal with ten minutes between meetings.

  • Participation by grade and classroom - who is in the habit, who needs a quieter on-ramp.
  • Themes of the week - what kinds of kindness are actually happening, in the students' own words.
  • Repair moments - conflicts that ended with a logged make-up act, visible without naming children.
  • Family reach - how many homes saw a weekly summary, the school's bridge to parents.
  • Safeguarding first: no public child profiles, no rankings of individual children, teacher review on anything flagged.
